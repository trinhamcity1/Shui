Why water

Shui (水) is the Chinese word for water. Water doesn't fight the rock in its path — it slows, splits, adapts, and keeps moving until it's on the other side. Given long enough, there's nowhere it can't reach.

That's the posture the mark should carry: not force, not a straight line punched through an obstacle, but persistence that looks calm from the outside. A learner staring down a hard lesson is doing exactly what water does to stone — finding the way through, one small yield at a time.

None of the six directions below try to draw the character 水 itself. They try to draw what it's describing.

Six directions

Each shown at full size, on light, on dark, and at app-icon scale

01 / THE DROP

The Drop

One drop, and inside it, the shape of watching — water and video content share a single mark.

02 / THE CURRENT

The Current

A single stroke, thinner where it lands than where it starts — Shui's own initial, drawn like it's still moving.

03 / THE REACH

The Reach

Open rings, not closed circles — water doesn't announce a boundary, it keeps extending past where you'd expect it to stop.

04 / THE WAY AROUND

The Way Around

An obstacle sits at the center. The line never touches it — it just keeps going, on the other side, like nothing was ever in the way.

05 / THE SOURCE

The Source

Two lines becoming one — echoing 水's own structure without ever spelling it out, built the way a river is actually built.

06 / THE VESSEL

The Vessel

A container with no fixed rim. Water always takes the shape it's poured into — so does a learner who stops fighting the lesson and adapts to it instead.

At app-icon scale

The test that actually matters — a thumb-sized square on a home screen

Drop · Current · Reach · Way Around · Source · Vessel

A starting opinion

The Current and The Vessel are the two that survive being shrunk to 40 pixels without losing the idea — that's the real test for an app icon, not how they look at this size. The Current reads instantly as motion and could double as a stroke inside the wordmark; the Vessel is the more emotionally specific of the two, closer to the actual sentence this all started from.

The Drop is the safest, most literal option if the instinct is "something a user recognizes in one glance, no explanation needed." The Reach and The Way Around are the most conceptually rich of the six and the hardest to defend at a glance — better suited to a splash screen or a loading state than a 40px icon.

Tell me which ones earn a second pass and I'll take them further — tighter geometry, a wordmark lockup, color variants for watermark use on generated video.
